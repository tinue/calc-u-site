Chapter 1 · Introduction

Calc-U-59

Calc-U-59 is an emulator of the TI-59, the TI-58 and the TI-58C, the programmable calculators Texas Instruments built from 1977. It runs on macOS, Windows, Linux, the iPhone and the iPad, and in the browser. It runs the calculators' original ROM through an emulated processor, at the original speed, so every result, the flashing overflow display and the documented quirks behave as on the real calculator. The display even looks as the eye saw the real one, dimming while the calculator works.

What Calc-U-59 emulates:

  • the TI-59, TI-58 and TI-58C, each with its memory partition and its state after switching on (Models and machine control, What differs by model);
  • all fourteen Solid State ROM modules, from the Master Library to the RPN Simulator (Solid State ROM modules);
  • magnetic cards for the TI-59, read and written as files (Magnetic cards);
  • the PC-100C print cradle, with its paper as dots and as text that can be copied (The PC-100C printer);
  • the TI-58C's constant memory, kept between starts (Constant memory);
  • a debugger at the level of program steps and of the processor's instructions, with traces for analysis afterwards (Debugger);
  • presets: plain text files that set up a calculator with a program, its registers, a cue card and a module in one go (Presets).

You can try it in the browser at calc-u.org/59/play/ before you install anything; how to install it on each system is in Installing Calc-U-59.

The three models

Model Introduced What it is
TI-59 (TI Programmable 59) May 1977 The top of the range: up to 960 program steps or 100 data registers, traded against each other in steps of 80 and 10, and a magnetic card reader in the side. Its memory is lost when it is switched off, unless the program is on a card
TI-58 (TI Programmable 58) May 1977 The same calculator with half the memory, up to 480 steps or 60 registers, and no card reader. Solid State ROM modules work as on the TI-59
TI-58C (TI Programmable 58C) 1979 A TI-58 with constant memory: programs and registers survive switching off

You choose the model in the model picker at the left of the control bar or under Machine ▸ Model ▸.

The calculators

The TI-59 was Texas Instruments' flagship programmable calculator, sold for around $300 from 24 May 1977. It followed the SR-52, and for the rest of the decade it was the calculator engineers, surveyors, navigators and financial analysts carried. You program it by recording keystrokes: in learn mode every key you press becomes a program step, and the ten user-defined keys, A to E and their second functions A′ to E′, are the entry points. The instruction set has conditionals, loops, subroutines and indirect register addressing.

These calculators use AOS, the Algebraic Operating System: you enter an expression roughly as it is written on paper, with up to nine levels of parentheses, and the calculator applies the precedence of the operators when you press =. The pending operations have edge cases that come out right only when the original ROM runs, which is what Calc-U-59 does. One of the modules, the RPN Simulator, makes the calculator behave like one with reverse Polish notation.

Magnetic cards and banks

The TI-59's card reader takes small magnetic strips; a side holds one bank, a fixed quarter of the 960-step memory: 240 steps or 30 data registers. Program steps fill the memory from the bottom, data registers from the top, so R00 is the last register of bank 4. n 2nd Op 17 moves the boundary between them in steps of ten registers (80 program steps), and a bank holds whatever falls into its quarter: steps, registers or both.

The display shows the partition as last step.last register; 479.59 (6 Op 17) is the TI-59's partition after switching on. A card side is also what the cue card is: once read, the real card slid into a slot above the keys, where the labels on its top edge named the keys A to E of the program you had loaded. Calc-U-59 shows that card above the keys too (Cue cards).

Modules and the printer

The TI-58 and TI-59 were the first handheld calculators with removable ROM program modules. A module held up to 5,000 program steps and ran straight from its ROM, leaving the user's memory free. The Master Library came in the box; Applied Statistics, Surveying, Aviation, Marine Navigation and the others were sold separately. Calc-U-59 has all fourteen.

The calculator docked into a thermal printer, the PC-100A, B or C. Beyond printing results, it listed a program with mnemonics instead of key codes, printed the data registers, and traced a program as it ran. Calc-U-59 emulates the PC-100C, its paper included.

For the original manuals, program libraries and the wider community, start at ti59.com and datamath.org. This help documents the emulator.
