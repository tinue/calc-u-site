Getting started

Printer and card reader

The PC-100C panel, paper strip, copy/cut, and saving and loading two-sided magnetic cards.

The printer view mimics the PC-100C. The PC-100C button at the top of the printer view, with its red or grey dot, connects or disconnects the printer; switching it resets the calculator, as plugging in the real printer does. Switch between dot view and text view depending on whether you want realism or easy copying.

PRINT and ADV are the printer's own keys: PRINT prints the display, ADV advances the paper while held. T is the printer's TRACE switch, which makes the calculator print every step it executes. Copy puts the strip on the clipboard as an image and as text; cut copies it and then clears the paper.

The printer runs at the real PC-100's speed, measured on a TI-59 and a TI-58C: a printed line takes about 0.29 s on the TI-59 (a 2nd List line 0.31 s), a blank line from ADV about 0.19 s. The printer runs on the calculator's clock, so on the TI-58C, whose clock is slower, everything prints about 15% slower, as on the real one. Programs that use the printer interrupt to cut a line short print the same dots as the real printer, faint dots included.

Holding the display runs the calculator at full speed, and that includes printing — a long 2nd List or a calendar printout finishes in moments.

Magnetic cards

The TI-59 has a card reader; the TI-58 and TI-58C don't. A magnetic card has two sides, as the real one has, and one card file holds both of them with one label.

Using a card takes two steps. Choosing a card only fits it into the calculator above the keys, where it labels them; nothing is read or written yet. Pressing one of its top corners then feeds that side through the reader: top-left for side 1, top-right for side 2. The corner lights up while the side passes.

Saving a program

  1. Press the Crd button with the plus sign and name the card. The fresh card fits above the keys, labeled with its name. If the name is taken, the app asks first: the card's first write replaces both sides and the label of the old one.
  2. Press 2nd Write for the bank you want to save: 1 2nd Write for bank 1.
  3. Press a corner of the card. Once the side has passed, it is saved in the file and the corner shows the bank it now holds.

A program too long for one side continues on the other: 2 2nd Write, then the other corner.

Loading it back

  1. Press the other Crd button and pick the card. On the Mac, Windows, Linux and in the browser you can also drop the file on the calculator.
  2. Press the corner of the side you want. The card stays fitted afterwards, so the other side is one press away.

Good to know

  • A side is not a bank. Either side can hold any bank, even both the same one. The number in a corner is the bank stored on that side, and follows a write; no number means the side is still empty.
  • Eject Card takes the place of the Crd buttons while a side is passing. It stops the pass; the card stays fitted.
  • A library program's cue card covers a fitted card, and the corners don't respond while it does. The card comes back with 2nd Pgm 00.
  • A fitted card goes when you fit another one, reset, switch the model, switch the printer or the module, or load a preset. The file stays as it is.
  • Older cards have one side and keep working: either corner feeds it, and it stays a one-sided card when written.
  • Only the side you write changes. The app reads the file again first, so the other side survives even if it was changed on another device meanwhile. A file it can't read is left alone, and the app reports it.

Where card files live

Card files (.U59) live in the app's card storage — iCloud on Mac, iPhone and iPad (or the app's local folder when iCloud is off), ~/Calc-U-59 on Windows and Linux. The picker lists only card files. In the browser emulator, cards last until you close the page: upload a .U59 file to fit it, and download a card to keep it. They are plain text, and a preset can feed them or label them; the format is in reference/StateFileFormat.md.
