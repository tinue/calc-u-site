TI-59 emulator, online

The calculator above is a working Texas Instruments TI-59 emulator running in your browser — no install, no account, nothing to download. It is the Calc-U 59 app's own emulator — the same code the Mac, Windows and Linux apps run — compiled to WebAssembly, executing the original Texas Instruments ROMs. The model picker switches between the TI-59, TI-58 and TI-58C, and the PC-100C printer is connected from the start. On a wide screen the printer and the full debugger sit beside the calculator, with the ASM overlay and trace downloads; on a phone the chevron in the corner turns to the printer's page and back.

Module 01 (Master Library) is loaded by default; Settings switches modules (that resets the calculator, as swapping the real module did) and chooses the calculator the page starts with. Preset loads one of the bundled presets or your own .ti59, .ti58 or .ti58c file, and you can also drop a file onto the calculator — files are read entirely in your browser and never leave your machine. The TI-58C keeps its constant memory in your browser between visits. For more programs to try, see the TI-59 Software Collection.

Press 2nd then Pgm then a two-digit program number to bring up that program's cue card, the same way it works on the module itself.

The TI-59 has a card reader. To save a program, press the Crd button for a fresh card, give the card a name, press 2nd Write (bank 1: 1 2nd Write), and press its top-left or top-right corner to write that side. To load it back, press the other Crd button and pick the card, or upload a .U59 card file, then press the corner of the side to read; Eject Card takes a passing card out (more on cards). Cards last until you close the page — download one from the card list to keep it.

The full app adds a card folder that stays (synced through iCloud on Apple devices), traces saved to disk and the Modernized LED style; it is free on the Mac and on Windows and Linux, or on the App Store for iPhone and iPad. On Android, install the web app from your browser. If you are not sure what any of this is, start here.

Prefer this without the browser chrome? Open the standalone app — the same calculator alone at its own address, installable to your phone's home screen for a one-tap, full-screen launch. Once installed it runs all three calculators offline.

Use your keyboard

Click the calculator once and it takes your keystrokes — a golden outline shows when it has them. Click anywhere else, or press Tab, and the page gets the keyboard back.

The number keys, the yellow operation keys, A–E, and EE ( ) are typeable. Everything else — 2nd, STO, RCL, LRN and the rest — is click-only, so the keyboard stays out of the way of the page. Holding Shift while you press a letter gives you that key's second function: Shift + A is A'.
