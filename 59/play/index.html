TI-59 emulator, online

The calculator above is a working Texas Instruments TI-59 emulator running in your browser — no install, no account, nothing to download. It is the Calc-U 59 app's own emulator — the same code the Mac, Windows and Linux apps run — compiled to WebAssembly, executing the original Texas Instruments ROMs. The model picker switches between the TI-59, TI-58 and TI-58C, and the PC-100C printer is connected from the start. On a wide screen the printer and the full debugger sit beside the calculator, with the ASM overlay and trace downloads; on a phone the chevron in the corner turns to the printer's page and back.

The menu bar above the calculator (on a phone: the More button) holds everything else. File ▸ Examples opens the bundled programs, File ▸ Load Preset… your own .ti59, .ti58 or .ti58c file, and you can also drop a file onto the calculator — files are read in your browser and never leave your machine. Machine switches the model and the peripherals, File ▸ Settings… the module. For more programs, see the TI-59 Software Collection.

Your magnetic cards and the TI-58C's constant memory stay in your browser from one visit to the next. File ▸ Import Media… brings a card file in, File ▸ Saved Media… lists, downloads and deletes your cards (more on cards).

Press 2nd then Pgm then a two-digit program number to bring up that program's cue card, the same way it works on the module itself.

The full app adds folders of your own for cards, presets and traces (synced through iCloud on the iPhone and the iPad); it is free for the Mac, Windows and Linux and on the App Store for iPhone and iPad. On Android, install the web app from your browser. The help explains all of it, and the introduction the three calculators.

Prefer this without the browser chrome? Open the standalone app — the same calculator alone at its own address, installable to your phone's home screen for a one-tap, full-screen launch. Once installed it runs all three calculators offline, with every module and every preset in its list.

Use your keyboard

Click the calculator once and it takes your keystrokes — a golden outline shows when it has them. Click anywhere else, or press Tab, and the page gets the keyboard back.

The number keys, the yellow operation keys, A–E, and EE ( ) are typeable. Everything else — 2nd, STO, RCL, LRN and the rest — is click-only, so the keyboard stays out of the way of the page. Holding Shift while you press a letter gives you that key's second function: Shift + A is A'.
